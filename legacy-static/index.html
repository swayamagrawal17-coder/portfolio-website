Most of my work starts the same way: a claim someone believes, and no data behind it. I go and collect it.

That has meant standing in nine market locations in Pimpri-Chinchwad with a questionnaire, asking 51 street vendors what actually happens when a UPI payment fails. It has meant reading a company's PP&E disclosures line by line to find where Ind AS 10 compliance quietly breaks. It has meant sitting inside a startup consulting firm and diagnosing why a client's leads cost too much.

I work in Excel, but the tool is the least interesting part. What matters is the step after the analysis: turning it into something a decision-maker can use. A 33-page report with seven named barriers. A tracker that cut prize distribution time in half for a 35-person team. A recommendation with a number attached to it.

Alongside that, I coordinate placement drives that encourage 100+ students to take part across 30+ companies, and event logistics for a 130-participant college fest. Work that is mostly invisible when it goes right. I graduate in 2027 and I'm looking for a management role in finance.
